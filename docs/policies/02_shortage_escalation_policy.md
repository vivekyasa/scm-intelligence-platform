# Shortage Escalation Policy

Document owner: Supply Planning. Applies to all items at all locations of Northwind Industrial Systems (a fictional company used for this portfolio project).

## Purpose

A shortage is a week where projected available quantity falls below zero. It means the plan expects to be unable to ship against demand. This policy sets how quickly a shortage must be acted on and who must be told.

## Severity levels

- HIGH: the projected shortage is larger than the item's safety stock quantity, or the item has no planned supply at all.
- MEDIUM: the projected shortage is smaller than the item's safety stock quantity.

## Response times

- Class A items with a HIGH shortage: the planner must act within 1 business day and notify the Supply Planning Manager.
- Class A items with a MEDIUM shortage: the planner must act within 2 business days.
- Class B items with a HIGH shortage: the planner must act within 2 business days.
- Class B items with a MEDIUM shortage and all Class C shortages: the planner must act within 5 business days.

Acting means choosing and recording one of the response options below, not necessarily resolving the shortage.

## Response options

1. Expedite an existing purchase order or production order, subject to the Expedite and Transfer Policy.
2. Transfer stock from another location that holds excess.
3. Place a new order, noting that the lead time may make it too late for the shortage week.
4. Re-allocate demand to a later week with the customer's agreement.
5. Accept the shortage and document the reason.

## Escalation

A shortage that is still open 3 business days before the shortage week must be escalated to the Supply Planning Manager. A shortage on a Class A item that is within 2 weeks of occurring and has no approved response is escalated to the Director of Operations.

## Closure

A shortage exception is closed when the projected available quantity for the affected week is back at or above zero, or when an accepted shortage has been documented and approved.
